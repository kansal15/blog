India has touched a billion Internet users and still growing. The mobile data consumption in India is among the highest in the world and I always wonder: "How many DNS queries do the billions of Internet-connected machines in India generate in a single day?"

Before your phone loads a page, System asks the DNS (like Sarvagya-1.10.10.10, Cloudflare, Google, Quad9, or any other resolver): "what is the IP address for this domain?" and this single question can trigger dozens of DNS queries.

Multiply this for billions of users, millions of IoT devices, millions of cloud servers, and hundreds of millions of other devices, and what you will get is the number of DNS queries generated in a day in India.

"Sarvagya — Bharat Public DNS (1.10.10.10 / 2409::1), operated jointly by NIC and NIXI, handles more than 5 billion DNS queries every single day — and it is just one of many resolvers serving vast number of Indian users."

Instead of aiming for a single, exact number, this study seeks to define a reliable and well-supported range for India's DNS traffic. We achieve this by cross-referencing four independent estimation models with actual operational data.

India crossed 1 billion broadband users in November 2025 — a historic milestone. [1] The wireless share of ~95% means the vast majority of DNS traffic originates from smartphones on Jio, Airtel, BSNL, and Vi networks, which fundamentally shapes how DNS resolvers are placed and handling the queries.

The daily active user figure of ~700 million applies a standard 68% DAU-to-subscriber ratio. This is the population that generates the bulk of human-driven DNS traffic on any given day.

Who resolves the DNS Queries ?

Every DNS query must go to a resolver. India has a layered resolver ecosystem — ISP-operated resolvers serving the majority, plus a growing sovereign public DNS and significant use of global public resolvers.

India DNS resolver landscape — APNIC Labs rvrs measurement[3]
Same ASN — user's resolver is in the same ISP network (Jio, Airtel, BSNL, Vi)~57%
Same Country — resolver in India, different ASN (like Sarvagya Public DNS)~35%
Google Public DNS (8.8.8.8) — out-of-country open resolver~7%
Cloudflare 1.1.1.1 — out-of-country open resolver~1%
All others (Quad9, OpenDNS, Sarvagya share of "same country", etc.)<1%

Source: APNIC Labs DNS Resolver Stats for India (IN). These are the actual figures from the APNIC rvrs measurement page — directly read from the APNIC dataset, not estimated. APNIC classifies resolvers by whether they share the same ASN as the user, same country code, or are a known open resolver. Total in-country DNS (same ASN + same country) = ~92%. This is markedly different from global averages and reflects India's uniquely dominant ISP resolver infrastructure (Jio + Airtel together serving ~48% + ~30% = 78% of APNIC-measured users). Note: Sarvagya — Bharat Public DNS (1.10.10.10 / 2409::1) is classified within the "Same Country" bucket in APNIC's rvrs data. Its exact share within that 35% bucket is not separately broken out, but its confirmed volume of 5B+ queries/day places it as a meaningful contributor within that category.

A critical note on APNIC's methodology: APNIC measures unique users observed via its ad-based sampling system during the measurement window. These are active measured users, not total billing subscribers. Jio's TRAI subscriber count (489M as of Dec 2025) [5] is higher than APNIC's 285.8M because APNIC captures only users who were actively observed generating DNS queries during the measurement period.

The Sarvagya — Bharat Public DNS Dataset: 5 billion+ queries per day

One of the most important real data point in this analysis. Sarvagya — Bharat Public DNS, operated by NIC and NIXI and accessible at 1.10.10.10 (IPv4) and 2409::1 (IPv6), handles more than 5 billion DNS queries every single day. This is not an estimate — it is confirmed operational data from the team that runs the service.

Its significance: Sarvagya is not Jio's resolver, not Airtel's, not Google DNS. It is India's sovereign public DNS service — handling a fraction of the total DNS requests. If this one resolver processes 5B+ queries daily, the aggregate across all of India's resolvers will be massive.

Human traffic: four independent estimation methods

Method 1 — Bottom-up: active users × queries per device per day

The most intuitive approach. The key question is: how many DNS queries does a typical Indian internet user's device generate per day?

The evidence here is complex and India-specific. The Cloudflare Radar 2025 Year in Review (December 2025) states that Cloudflare responds to approximately 67 million DNS queries per second (authoritative + resolver combined) — equating to 5.79 trillion DNS queries per day globally. [7] An earlier 2023 Application Security Report had cited 25 million QPS (2.1T/day), confirming the network has grown 2.7× in two years. [6]

For per-device benchmarks DNSFilter's research puts the average at roughly 5,000 DNS queries per device per day (in its 2025 Annual Security Report)— but this is measured primarily on enterprise networks in the US and Europe, where managed endpoints, Active Directory lookups, SaaS platform telemetry, and MDM agents generate continuous background traffic. India's user profile is fundamentally different:

India mobile user profile:
- ~95% of internet access via smartphone (single device, no AD, no MDM)
- Jio/Airtel aggressive CGNAT: many users share one resolver session
- Cloudflare consumer research: smartphones avg 1,000–2,000 DNS q/day
- India-calibrated estimate: ~1,500 q/device/day (mobile)
- Fixed broadband users (Airtel BB, ACT, Hathway): ~3,000 q/device/day
(multiple home devices, smart TVs, higher engagement)

Applying these rates to 700M daily active users with an average of 1.2 devices per user (GSMA India data): 700M × 1.2 × ~1,500 ≈ 1.26 trillion queries/day from human-driven mobile traffic, with fixed broadband adding another contribution.

Method 2 — Top-down: India's share of global DNS traffic

Global authoritative DNS servers handle approximately 330–350 billion queries per day. Cloudflare's DDoS Threat Report Q2 2023 confirms handling over 2 trillion DNS queries per day on its own network alone. [8]

India accounts for approximately 18–20% of Asia-Pacific internet traffic, and APAC represents roughly 22% of global internet users — placing India at approximately 4–5% of the world's DNS volume. Authoritative DNS gives us 15–17 billion queries/day for India. Recursive DNS (what users actually generate at their resolvers) runs 10–15× authoritative traffic due to TTL expiry and cache churn. Adding ISP resolver traffic: 800B–2T.
