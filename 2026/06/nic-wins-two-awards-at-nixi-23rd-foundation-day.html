NIC Wins Two Awards at NIXI's 23rd Foundation Day

  • Post by Gaurav Kansal
  • June 22, 2026

I am happy to share that the National Informatics Centre (NIC) received two awards at NIXI's 23rd Foundation Day on 19 June 2026. I received the awards on behalf of NIC.

The Two Awards

  • Best Resource Managed Account Award: for deploying RPKI in the BGP routing of Internet traffic.
  • Internet Service Provider Award: for sending the highest Content Delivery Network (CDN) traffic to NIXI.

What Is RPKI?

Resource Public Key Infrastructure (RPKI) is a cryptographic security framework designed to make the Internet's routing system safer by verifying who is allowed to announce specific IP addresses.

Networks on the Internet use BGP to tell each other which IP addresses they can reach. BGP trusts what it hears. So a wrong announcement, by mistake or on purpose, can send traffic to the wrong place. This is called a route hijack or a route leak.

RPKI adds a check. The holder of an IP address block publishes a signed record, called a ROA (Route Origin Authorisation). It says which network is allowed to announce that block. Other networks can check routes against these records and drop the ones that do not match.

RPKI at NIC and NKN

I led the RPKI deployment for NIC and the National Knowledge Network (NKN). We signed our IP address space with ROAs. Networks that check RPKI can now confirm that our routes really come from us, and drop fake ones. This makes Government services on NICNET and NKN harder to hijack.

Update (July 2026): I have since published an RPKI live statistics page. It shows RPKI adoption across the world and in India, and refreshes regularly.

CDN Traffic and NIXI

NIXI (National Internet Exchange of India) runs Internet exchange points, where networks in India swap traffic directly. The second award recognises that NIC sent the highest CDN traffic to NIXI. Keeping this traffic inside India makes the Internet faster for users.

Thank You

Thanks to NIXI for the recognition. Thanks also to my colleagues at NIC and NKN, who made this work possible.
